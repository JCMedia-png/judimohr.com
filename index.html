Questions women ask me

Getting started

What happens in the complimentary conversation?

We’ll sit down, talk through where you are and where you want to go, and you’ll leave with a clear picture of where you stand. No pressure, no jargon, just clarity.

So how does working with you work?

We start with an insight meeting, all about your dreams and your fears, then a fact find. Then I build your financial plan and your cash flow, so you can see where you stand. We look at different versions of that plan together, then talk about the kinds of investments and other resources that can support it. After that, we review regularly.

What do you help with?

Retirement readiness, investment management, retirement income, Social Security, tax-minimization strategies, insurance, long-term care, estate planning coordination, and financial organization. I look at everything together, because your money doesn’t live in five separate boxes, and your plan shouldn’t either.

Do I need to have everything organized first?

No. You do not need to solve your whole financial life today. Start with the question that is creating the most uncertainty. Finding everything and getting it organized can be the first thing we do together.

Do I have to live near Tega Cay?

No. I work with women in Tega Cay, Fort Mill, Indian Land, Rock Hill, Clover, York and the greater Charlotte area, and beyond. I can discuss securities business with residents of Delaware, Florida, Georgia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee and Texas.

Is this for me?

My husband has always handled the money. Is this for me?

Yes. You’re not alone, and you’re not behind. A good place to start is knowing where the accounts are held, how bills are paid, who the professional contacts are, where passwords and documents are stored, and what income would continue if your spouse died.

I was recently widowed. Where do I start?

Grief and financial urgency do not mix well. Separate immediate decisions from decisions that can wait, gather documents, understand cash flow, and avoid making irreversible choices too quickly.

I’m divorcing after 50. Can you help?

Retirement accounts, Social Security, pensions, housing, taxes, insurance, and future income all matter, not simply the dollar value of the assets on a settlement sheet.

I’ve inherited money. What should I do first?

Before investing inherited money, understand taxes, account type, your own goals, near-term needs, and whether the inheritance changes your retirement plan.

I already work with an advisor. Can I still talk with you?

Yes. You deserve a place to ask questions, understand your choices, and make thoughtful decisions without pressure.

Is this only for women?

My practice is built around women approaching and navigating retirement. I work with women and their families.

What to expect

Have I saved enough to retire?

Start with spending, income, debt, investment risk, cash reserves, health care, and the gap between what you want and what your resources can reasonably support.

How do I replace my paycheck?

Coordinate Social Security, pensions, retirement accounts, taxable assets, and cash so the income plan is not built around one account alone.

Will my money last?

Market declines, inflation, taxes, long-term care, unexpected family needs, and living longer than expected all deserve a place in the plan.

What should I do five years before retirement?

That window is often ideal for testing retirement spending, reviewing investment risk, evaluating Roth conversion opportunities, and planning the transition from accumulation to distribution.

How often will we review my plan?

Regularly, because life changes and your plan should keep up. By the end, you understand your money well enough to make decisions you feel good about.

The things women worry about

What if I don’t know the right questions to ask?

You’re not bad with money. Nobody ever took the time to explain it to you in a way that made sense. That’s what I’m here for. First, I listen: what’s most important to you, and what needs to be solved.

I’ve been talked down to by advisors before. Will this be different?

I put the options on the table. We talk through them, and we decide together what fits and what doesn’t. No pressure, no jargon, just clarity.

Am I invested the right way for me?

You do not need to become an investment expert. You do need to understand what you own, why you own it, how much risk you are taking, and how your portfolio fits the retirement you want.

Am I paying more tax than I need to?

IRAs, Roth IRAs, 401(k)s, and taxable accounts are taxed differently. How assets are distributed among accounts can matter as much as what you own.

I don’t work for a big bank or financial institution. I work for the people who choose to become my clients. As a CFP® professional, I’m required by the CFP Board to act as a fiduciary when I give financial advice, which means the advice must be in your best interest.

Why I do this

I’m Judi Lohr Mohr. My dad was the breadwinner. My mom stayed home. When my dad was 55, he was downsized and forced to retire. They moved to a retirement community in Florida, and my mom watched couple after couple with two Social Security checks and two pensions. She kept thinking, what did we do wrong?
